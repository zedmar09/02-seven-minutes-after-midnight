# Seven Minutes After Midnight

Genre: Boys' love / adult men / time-slip romance / historical mystery / emotional thriller / manga-manhwa-manhua inspired

Premise:
Daniel Soriano, a present-day museum conservator, discovers an abandoned railway cafe that mysteriously returns to life for exactly seven minutes every midnight.

Inside, he meets Tomas Rivera, a cheerful young baker working in the same cafe forty years in the past. Tomas is instantly recognizable by his long dark hair, usually tied loosely behind his head while he works. Stray strands constantly fall across his face as he kneads dough, serves coffee, and leans across the counter to speak with Daniel.

Although they can see and touch the same counter, Daniel and Tomas cannot cross completely into each other's time. They can only exchange letters, pastries, photographs, and small objects through the cafe's service window. Their seven-minute meetings gradually become the most important part of their lives.

While restoring an old newspaper recovered from the railway archives, Daniel discovers that Tomas died during a mysterious station fire. He attempts to warn him, but every effort to change history weakens their connection. Seven minutes becomes six, then five.

Worse, Daniel uncovers evidence that the fire was deliberately started, and that the long-haired baker he has fallen in love with may have been its intended target.

## Tone Lock

This story should feel like a mature BL time-slip romance with historical mystery pressure: intimate midnight conversations, adults carrying grief quietly, old rail timetables, handwritten letters, flour on sleeves, dark platform glass, museum conservation labs, microfilm rooms, and the dread of finding a loved one's death already printed in an archive.

Keep the romance emotionally adult and restrained. Daniel and Tomas fall in love through seven-minute meetings, exchanged objects, careful jokes, impossible tenderness, and the growing terror that every warning may destroy the only bridge between them.

Avoid school romance energy, chibi comedy, fantasy palace overlap, gothic coffin revenge, supernatural dead-train mythology, corporate revenge systems, loyalty-number overlays, and simple time-travel wish fulfillment. The cafe is not a portal anyone can freely use. It is a fragile wound between two specific nights.

## Visual Direction

Use elegant black-and-white manga, Korean manhwa, and light Chinese manhua visual language if this story is adapted later: expressive adult male faces, long quiet close-ups, steam and window reflections, detailed hands, period cafe uniforms, handwritten letters, archival textures, railway architecture, rain-dark platforms, and suspenseful panel pacing.

The visual world should feel like a grounded railway romance thriller, not fantasy royalty or supernatural station mythology. The station is physical and historical: chipped tile, shuttered ticket counters, brass menu boards, cafe stools, service windows, old fans, pastry cases, archival boxes, scorch marks, newsprint, and a clock that refuses to move normally after midnight.

## Character Look Lock

`Seven Minutes After Midnight` must not reuse the visual silhouettes from the existing story library.

- Daniel Soriano must not resemble Kang Jiho, Kang Do-yoon, Lucien Veyr, Ren Calder, Miharu Ren, Shen Mo, or Li Yue. No round-glasses accident-survivor look, no ash-brown corporate undercut, no black mourning cloak, no courier braids, no teenage station uniform, no palace robes, and no half-mask.
- Tomas Rivera must remain visually distinct from every existing long-haired male lead. His long dark hair is loose, warm, practical, and tied for baking work, not aristocratic, warlord-like, xianxia, ghostly, or school-mystery styling.
- The railway cafe must not visually merge with `Lanterns of the Last Station`. This story has no dead passengers, no paper lantern rules, no hidden Platform 13, no upward rain, no ticket-punch magic, and no supernatural conductor. Its time-slip identity is built around a service window, a cafe counter, objects aging across time, and a station fire investigation.

## Midnight Cafe Rules

1. The abandoned railway cafe returns to life from 12:00 AM to 12:07 AM each night.
2. Daniel experiences the cafe from 2026. Tomas experiences the cafe from 1986.
3. During the seven minutes, both men can see and touch the same counter surface, but neither can fully cross into the other's time.
4. The service window is the only reliable exchange point for small objects.
5. Objects passed from 1986 to 2026 arrive aged by forty years unless protected by the seven-minute interval. Paper yellows, ink fades, pastries dry, photographs warp, and metal tarnishes.
6. Objects passed from 2026 to 1986 look unnervingly new, then begin adapting to the older time once the cafe closes.
7. Living bodies cannot pass through the service window. A hand may touch through the opening, but pressure, breath, and heartbeat become unstable near the threshold.
8. Direct warnings about major historical events weaken the connection. The more Daniel tries to change Tomas's death, the shorter the seven-minute window becomes.
9. Indirect clues, coded letters, and ordinary objects cause less damage than spoken warnings, but the cafe seems to understand intent.
10. The cafe clock records the remaining connection. Once the minute hand loses a full minute, it does not recover easily.
11. The fire at San Aurelio Junction is a fixed point in the archive, but the archive may be incomplete, falsified, or deliberately damaged.
12. If Tomas survives by changing the past too strongly, the cafe may never become abandoned, meaning Daniel may never find it and may lose every memory made there.
