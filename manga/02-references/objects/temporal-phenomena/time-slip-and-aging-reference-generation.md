## Purpose

Create a reusable black-and-white effect reference that makes the time connection readable through material contrast, aligned architecture, steam, dust, light cutoff, and object aging. The visual language must support intense manga staging without becoming a generic glowing portal.

Deferred dependencies: approved Cafe Siete perspective, time-transfer props, and clock/sign/door references. Each must be resolved to an actual approved path and SHA-256 before release.

## Generation Brief

Produce ONE 1536 x 1024 landscape PNG named `time-slip-and-aging-reference-v001.png`. Draw a professional black-and-white manga phenomenon reference sheet with exactly five isolated, left-to-right studies. Use the same counter wood, hatch sill, bread, saucer, and paper designs from the future approved dependencies.

The five studies are:

1. Dormant boundary: ruined 2026 counter, cold darkness, dust, broken furniture, and no visible supernatural effect.
2. Active connection at the counter: 1986 warmth, steam, and clean counter surface align sharply with the 2026 ruin, while Daniel's surrounding stools, tile, painted door, and darkness remain damaged. Use slight line-registration tension and a hard material threshold, not a portal ring.
3. Unstable shared possession: the tray or saucer spans the boundary and remains materially fresh while held from both eras. Show subtle doubled edge registration and opposing steam/dust motion, with no aging yet.
4. Completed forward transfer: the same roll and saucer immediately after release into 2026, with bread shrunken, dark, cracked, and flaking; sugar dulled; saucer rim yellowed; and one hairline glaze fracture.
5. Closure: the 1986 view vanishes into the ordinary ruined cafe, rain and cold return, the charred hatch remains, and the returned notebook page finishes yellowing after clearing the opening.

## Visual Grammar

- Keep shared architecture perfectly aligned across eras. The uncanny effect comes from contradictory material states occupying one composition, not from warped perspective.
- Differentiate 1986 and 2026 with line density, flat screentone value, steam contours versus dust texture, and crisp versus damaged surfaces. Do not simulate warm/cold color, luminous light, or cinematic deep-black exposure.
- Light, heat, sound cues, steam, and limited object contact may cross. Daniel's present-day floor, stools, painted door, and ruin do not restore.
- Backward paper remains crisp. Forward objects age only after complete transfer and release.
- A brief wrist contact may read as sharp pain through a small white impact break or tense line vibration, but it causes no wound, burn, lightning, or visible permanent damage.
- Closure is abrupt and materially quiet after the final click. Do not make it an explosion.

Create a finished clean black-and-white human-drawn 2D manga production sketch/reference sheet on white paper. Use precise matched contours, small flat solid-black shapes, restrained discrete screentone, sparse functional hatching, dry-brush dust texture, white-paper steam shapes, tactile aging cracks, and controlled vibration lines only where they explain the transition. Keep all five studies isolated and equally readable rather than arranging them as a cinematic sequence.

More resolved than a rough thumbnail or construction sketch, less atmospheric than a story panel. No rough construction residue, color, tint, sepia, neon, chromatic aberration, holograms, magic circles, runes, lightning, star fields, cosmic tunnels, floating clock fragments, ghosts, glow, bloom, rim light, particle storms, cinematic exposure, vignette, lens effects, bokeh, blur, smooth gradient, airbrush, painterly grayscale, photorealism, CGI, or 3D rendering.

- No full-room restoration in 2026.

- No fire vision, death image, culprit clue, or future-event montage.
