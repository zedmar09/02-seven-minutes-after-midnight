# Reconstruction Options v001

Status: proposed. The audit is approved; none of these options, the new policy, or the revision plan is approved or active.

## Option A: Conservative Continuity Repair

Preserve essentially the current Chapter 1 wording, sequence, and the seven-arc aspiration. Correct the demonstrable adaptation-name changes, restore the missing introductions/question links, reconcile clock/sleeve/flashlight descriptions, and add the smallest physical bridge needed to use the window. Keep broader transfer, cost, calendar, and memory questions open until a later architecture pass.

Improves immediate consistency and reduces disruption. It preserves the most literal author text and may make a useful quick opening correction. It risks postponing the same rule decisions until more chapters depend on them, especially the protection exception and after-closure reply. It does not by itself demonstrate that 105 chapters have distinct purposes.

Use when the author values minimal alteration over resolving the broader foundations now. Even this option must not turn unknown mechanics into assumed canon or treat missing chapters as reviewed.

## Option B: Balanced Repair, Recommended

Preserve Daniel, Tomas, their adult ages, occupational identities, the chapter title, the core sequence, chosen family, intimate object exchanges, and the restrained voice. Make localized prose repairs around the physical hatch and overlap, the trapped reply, justified late access, guessed year, and evidence ordering. Specify a proposed private transfer model and baseline cost/calendar boundaries. Prepare independent-goal and revelation-ladder proposals before continuation.

Use the supplied Chapter 1 as the only immediate prose base. Keep the first fifteen titles as a provisional envelope and the opening three-chapter sequence as a development checkpoint. Later chapters, other cafes, memory-reset mechanisms, exact fire chronology, and the ending mechanism remain conditional until they have approved architecture.

Improves the greatest concentration of audit concerns without replacing the story's identity. It addresses the fifteen P1 findings through either an immediate repair or an explicit gated action, and retains all P2/P3 issues in the work plan. The tradeoff is that several recommendations are genuinely new creative choices: they narrow undefined exceptions and add small causal details. Those choices are enumerated in decision-proposals-v001.md for approval, not smuggled into canon.

This is the preferred option because the supplied opening already achieves a distinctive voice and emotional progression. Its weaknesses are concentrated in links, mechanics, and continuation architecture; the audit does not support discarding the core chapter.

## Option C: Transformative Restructure

Keep only the central two-adults/seven-minutes premise, chosen motifs, and grounded station setting as nonnegotiable material. Design a self-contained season around a selected ending first, advance fire evidence and Tomas's independent investigation earlier, substantially reorder or combine the existing title sequence, and remove other-cafe or repeated-memory arcs unless indispensable. Do not prescribe a chapter count before scene purposes justify it.

Improves the ability to build one tightly unified rescue/mystery arc and can substantially reduce long-series risk. It also has the highest voice and continuity cost: more new scenes, altered reveal order, changed chapter architecture, and likely a larger adaptation rebuild. No evidence in the one supplied chapter currently requires this degree of change.

Use only if the author chooses a different project scale or if later approved architecture shows the current plan cannot deliver the desired ending within sustainable capacity.
