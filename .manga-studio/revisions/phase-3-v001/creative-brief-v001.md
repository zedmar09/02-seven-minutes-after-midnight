## Core Premise and Reader Promise

The source premise follows Daniel Soriano, a museum conservator in 2026, and Tomas Rivera, a baker in 1986, who meet for seven minutes through a railway cafe. The larger planned conflict is that saving Tomas from a recorded station fire may destroy the connection through which they fall in love. The supplied first chapter establishes the meeting and danger, not proof of Tomas's death or guilt in the fire.

Proposed reader promise: two adult men make something real through brief meetings, work, humor, and exchanged objects. Each attempt to understand the past or protect one another changes what they can know, risk, and choose together.

The proposed thematic focus is preservation versus agency: keeping evidence is not the same as keeping a person safe, and loving someone requires respecting choices that cannot be controlled. This is an interpretation recommended for development, not a claim that the author has already declared a message.

## Creative Priorities

1. Make Daniel's professional habits cause discoveries and reveal emotion. A change in how he handles an object can matter more than a paragraph explaining his feelings.
2. Preserve Tomas's warmth and independent initiative. Develop an obligation to the cafe and its people that can make him disagree with a rescue shortcut.
3. Keep both men active in the relationship and investigation. Future changes in memory must not replace free choice or erase all consequences of prior trust.
4. Give clues a clear path from observation to hypothesis to corroboration. Daniel must not know a name, year, death, or crime before the scene supplies a reason.
5. Let limitations create choices. Separate ordinary elapsed time from permanent connection loss, shared contact from completed transfer, and a delayed fall of paper from a fresh post-closure crossing.
6. Let quiet moments breathe. Do not require a cliffhanger, equal arc length, or dramatic panel arrangement when an earned emotional turn serves the scene.
