## Definition of Success

The first success is a complete, recognizable, internally clear opening: two adults with distinct voices, objects whose changes the reader can follow, and a connection whose limits make choices matter. The first development success is a coherent opening sequence in which every meeting changes knowledge, agency, danger, or intimacy. The eventual story must pay off both the relationship and the fire investigation using established constraints.

Commercial success is not presently measurable: no platform, audience history, budget, sales data, or release capacity was supplied. Do not substitute a 105-chapter count, number of generated pages, or follower target for completed story value.

## Feedback Checkpoints

1. After the exact Chapter 1 reconstruction is approved for review, the author checks voice and physical/knowledge continuity against the preserved source. Keep all notes tied to passage and issue IDs.
2. Only after explicit sharing authorization, invite a small group of approximately five voluntary adult readers to read the same approved version. Use private/offline handling consistent with the no-upload rule. Ask what each lead knows, how objects cross, and what the final warning proves; record confusion in the reader's own words.
3. At the opening-sequence checkpoint, ask whether each episode changed something and whether Tomas made a consequential choice. Treat repeated concerns as revision signals, not as proof of universal reader preference.
4. At approved storyboard/lettering review, check the same facts through the reading sequence and distinguish script clarity from rendered clarity. No rendered results can be inferred now.
5. Before a public release decision, review measured capacity, buffer, creator satisfaction, collaborator readiness, and any authorized reader feedback together.

No recruitment, message, upload, or outside review is authorized by proposing these checkpoints. An author-only pass remains useful while sharing is prohibited.

## Three Metrics

| Metric | Operational definition | Proposed checkpoint threshold | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core comprehension | Share of authorized test readers who correctly identify the eras, the small-object exchange/aging effect, and the difference between a warning and proof of murder | At least 4 of 5 correctly explain all three after Chapter 1; repeated identical confusion triggers review | A tiny convenience sample is diagnostic only; no readership or market estimate |
| Meaningful scene change | Share of reviewed scenes with an observable before/after change in at least one of conflict, knowledge, relationship, emotion, goal, or world state | Every scene in the reconstructed unit has a specific change recorded; do not count a label without a textual event | An internal craft check does not measure enjoyment; a quiet emotional change counts |
| Sustainable workload | Planned work for a release interval divided by the creator's voluntarily available work time, checked against actual logged effort | Target at or below 80%, with the proposed two-unit buffer maintained over two completed intervals before increasing cadence | Capacity is currently unknown; external delays and revision load must be included, and no medical productivity claim is made |

All thresholds are proposed working rules, not measured outcomes. Do not manufacture reader responses, baseline scores, hours, or retention numbers. Use author satisfaction with voice as a veto rather than turning it into a misleading numerical quality score.

## Small Single-Variable Experiments

Experiment A, after approved prose variants and sharing permission: compare the existing order of the two opening evidence observations with one alternative order, keeping the wording and every other passage fixed. Use separate first-read groups where feasible. Compare the core-comprehension metric and the location of confusion; record preference comments without treating them as statistical proof. Do not simultaneously alter the title, ending, length, and question wording.

Experiment B, at the later approved storyboard stage: move only the first Tomas reveal from a page-end partial reveal to the next-page full reveal while holding story text, artwork inputs, and other layout choices constant. Use geometry-only comparisons before any new art, and measure comprehension plus qualitative anticipation. This is a proposed later experiment, not permission to storyboard or generate artwork now.

Run one experiment at a time. If the sample is too small to distinguish a benefit, keep the version that preserves voice and causes less downstream work. Never manufacture an A/B result or pick a winner from intuition while labeling it tested.

## Pivot Criteria

- If two authorized review rounds still show the same central confusion, revise the relevant rule presentation or clue order before expanding the cast or adding another cafe.
- If scenes repeatedly lack a meaningful change, compress or combine proposed beats before adding chapters to reach 105.
- If Tomas's choices never alter Daniel's plan, revise the independent-goal architecture before using a rescue climax.
- If actual effort repeatedly exceeds the 80% planning allowance or consumes the release buffer, reduce frequency, episode size, or concurrent work. Do not promise public dates against unknown capacity.
- If memory changes repeatedly erase earned relationship progress, consolidate the reset mechanism or change the arc structure.
- If expanded cafes displace San Aurelio and the couple, defer those arcs. If expansion proves essential through approved cause-and-effect architecture, revisit scope in a new plan version.
- If the author no longer recognizes the voice, reject the offending revision even if it improves a local clarity metric.

These are decision triggers, not automatic edits, cancellations, or commitments.
