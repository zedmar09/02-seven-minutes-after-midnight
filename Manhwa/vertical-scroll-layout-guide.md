# Vertical Scroll Layout Guide

## One Chapter, One Scroll

Finished chapter is one centered continuous vertical reader. Technical strips and V-beat IDs are production-only and never reader-visible.

## Width Lock

Use one fixed generation width for all technical strips. Stitch approved strips first; uniformly resize only the completed master if needed.

## Canonical Environment Anchor

Every view must be physically possible inside approved environment canon. Preserve structural footprint, counter, service window, clock/menu-wall relationship, doors/openings, station routes, world-space scale, and truthful reverse angles.

The 1986 and 2026 views are historical states of the same structure, not independent redesigns.

## Read Beats Are Not Labels

Never render V/strip/panel/scene/shot/QA/temp-state headings, technical headers, page numbers, crop guides, or production notes.

## Adjacent-Slice Continuity

New slice = new camera/read beat, not reset. Preserve unmodified pose, facing, hands, held items, accessories, prop positions, clock minute, era state, object age state, lighting source, and environment geography.

## Camera Axis / Eyeline

Same uninterrupted Daniel/Tomas conversation preserves physical sides and gaze targets around the shared counter/service window. Reverse shots may flip screen-left/right but not world-space sides. Cross the 180° axis only with a neutral reorientation view when necessary.

## Perspective

Use ordinary human-eye/manhwa perspective. Reject fisheye, extreme lens warping, forced perspective that changes body/object scale, or camera tricks that fake threshold contact.

## Mobile Evidence Legibility

Critical proof must read at normal phone width. If a wide is insufficient, use an adjacent same-object detail with ownership/context.

Chapter 1 proof includes token retrieval, shared tray contact, service-window opening, pastry crossing/aging, archival note crossing, notebook warning crossing, returned aged page, and final scratched fire warning.

## Crop / Gutter / Divider Safety

Do not place decisive hands, notes, token, tray, service-window contact, clock time, aging proof, or warning text exactly on crop/seam/divider edges.

## Depth / Contact

Flat overlap is not enough. Fingers actually touch/grip, objects actually cross the threshold, Daniel and Tomas occupy plausible opposite-era positions, and carrier text remains physically attached to page/glass/clock.

## Flexible Materials / Gravity

Hair, apron cloth, paper, notes, pastry packets, towels, straps, rain, and falling/rolling objects obey gravity and support. No floating props or clipping through solid architecture.

## Time-Slip Reader Order

The scroll must follow exact causal order. A later clock minute cannot appear above an earlier minute. A transformed object result cannot appear before the crossing action. A returned aged page cannot appear before the connection closes.

## Dual-Era Separation

Era readability comes from environment condition, practical lighting, clothing/objects, and occupancy—not cinematic filters or portal VFX. When both eras appear in one composition, make the structural correspondence clear while keeping each side's state legible.

## Black Read-Slice Grammar

Use compact dark gutters/dividers where helpful for pacing. Larger gaps require story function: suspense, time transition, silence, sound-first beat, or controlled reveal. Never create giant filler bands.

## Narration / Dialogue Placement

Place text according to scenario and causal order. Never cover decisive contact, clock proof, service-window geometry, age transformation, or identity-critical face/hand cues.

## No Traditional Page Artifacts

No printed-page grids as the governing format, no repeated equal boxes down the entire scroll, and no reader-visible page numbering.
