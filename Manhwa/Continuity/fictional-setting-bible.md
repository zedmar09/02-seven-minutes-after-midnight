# Fictional Setting Bible — Absolute

Status: **BINDING STORY-WORLD GEOGRAPHY AUTHORITY.**

`Seven Minutes After Midnight` takes place in a **fully fictional setting**. The story must not be anchored to, identified with, or visually presented as any real country, real city, real province/state, or famous real-world location.

## Canonical Geography

### Caligo Vale

`CALIGO VALE` is the broader fictional independent rail territory in which the story takes place.

- It has no canonical real-country counterpart.
- Do not state or imply that Caligo Vale is inside a real nation or continent.
- Do not assign a real-world flag, map silhouette, currency, postal system, telephone format, license-plate format, national railway logo, or government seal.
- If broader geography is required later, invent it inside this continuity system before using it in story or visual production.

### San Aurelio

`SAN AURELIO` is a fictional municipality and historic railway hub inside Caligo Vale.

Canonical local institutions/places include:
- `San Aurelio Junction` — the historic railway station
- `Cafe Siete` — cafe attached to the old ticket-hall zone
- `San Aurelio Municipal Museum` — local civic museum
- `San Aurelio Railway Archive` — museum restoration/archive project tied to the station

These names are story-world canon. They are not references to real municipalities, stations, museums, or countries.

## No Real-Location Leakage — Absolute

Reader-facing story, character canon, environment prompts, covers and rendered artwork must not introduce:
- real country names
- real city names
- real province/state names
- real nationalities or ethnic-national compound labels as geographic shorthand
- recognizable real flags or coats of arms
- real transit/railway logos
- real government agency names
- readable real maps
- famous skylines or landmark replicas
- recognizable one-to-one copies of a famous railway station
- real license plates, postal codes, phone-number formats or civic seals

A generic object may look historically plausible, but it must belong to the fictional Caligo Vale / San Aurelio world.

## Character Identity Rule

Character names, surnames, skin tones, facial features, hair, clothing and food traditions do **not** establish a real-world nationality unless creator canon explicitly creates a fictional local identity.

For visual canon:
- describe the character directly by physical traits
- do not add real-country flags or nationality badges
- do not infer a real nationality from surnames
- do not label Daniel, Tomas, Maribel or other cast members with real-world nationality combinations

Characters are residents/people of the fictional Caligo Vale setting unless a future fictional birthplace is added here.

## Architecture / Culture Rule

The world may use a believable invented blend of historic railway, municipal, cafe and conservation design language. It must remain internally coherent rather than copied one-to-one from a real city or nation.

San Aurelio's recurring visual identity remains:
- arched railway windows
- green-and-cream tile relationships
- iron structural members
- brass signage language
- ticket windows
- platform clocks
- stained-glass transoms where established
- rain gutters
- Cafe Siete beside the ticket hall

These are **San Aurelio canon**, not permission to reproduce a real station.

## Production-Terminology Exception

`Korean manhwa` / `webtoon` may remain in repository production documents **only as visual-format/style terminology**. It is not a statement that the story occurs in Korea or any real location.

## Future Location Gate

Before any future chapter introduces a new named city, district, port, station, municipality or broader territory:
1. confirm it is fictional
2. add it to this file
3. define its relationship to Caligo Vale / San Aurelio
4. ensure no existing environment or dialogue authority contradicts it
5. only then use it in story/visual production

Any accidental real-location insertion is a continuity blocker and must be corrected before rendering.
