# Manhwa Style Guide

## Core Target — ABSOLUTE

**STRICT FLAT 2D HUMAN-DRAWN FULL-COLOR KOREAN MANHWA/WEBTOON ILLUSTRATION.**

Every generated or edited story image must unmistakably read as a flat 2D illustration made with intentional human-drawn linework. This is a hard production gate, not a mood suggestion.

Required:
- clean intentional line art
- stable line weight and readable silhouettes
- flat colors
- restrained simple hard-edged cel shading only when useful
- matte skin, hair, fabrics, tile, wood, paper, brass, glass, steel, ceramic, pastry, rain-wet clothing, and station materials
- believable adult proportions according to canon
- natural hands and one coherent body per character
- stable permanent facial/body landmarks
- stable character local colors/materials
- stable project palette
- mobile-first readability

Do **not** make the project richer, more cinematic, more painterly, more glossy, or more realistic than the approved flat-2D canon.

Reject:
- photorealism or semi-photorealism
- 3D/CGI/game-render appearance
- plastic/glossy/wet-looking skin or hair
- beauty-ad shine
- mirror-polished brass/tile/glass used as glamour
- soft airbrush rendering
- heavy gradients that erase flat shape design
- painterly realism
- cinematic color grading
- cinematic depth-of-field blur
- bloom, lens flare, excessive rim light
- dramatic film lighting not required by the story
- hyper-detailed pores/skin texture
- over-rendered eyes/lips/hair
- generic AI-polished glossy finish

## Character Canonical Authority — Absolute

Approved canonical character-card WebPs under `../Character-References/` are absolute identity authority.

They control:
- face geometry and age presentation
- eye/nose/jaw relationships
- permanent marks/scars and anatomical placement
- hair length/part/tie/fringe/silhouette/color
- height/build relationships
- wardrobe construction and signature local colors
- permanent accessories/wearables

A close-up, reverse shot, rain scene, warm cafe light, flashlight scene, dramatic crop, or previous generated strip never outranks the character WebP.

### Daniel Soriano Locks

- exactly age 36 in 2026
- warm brown skin
- long narrow adult face
- close-cropped black curls with early silver at temples
- stable nose-bridge scar
- thin rectangular amber-tinted work glasses when established
- medium-tall slim specialist build
- slate-blue chore jacket work identity

Reject teenage/idol recast, round-glasses identity, undercut, long hair, hyper-muscular body, or lost/moved scar.

### Tomas Rivera Locks

- exactly age 28 in 1986
- sun-warm tan skin
- long dark wavy hair past shoulders
- low practical work tie at nape/back with jade-green cord
- loose face-framing strands

Reject short hair, high ponytail, fantasy bun, aristocratic/xianxia/ghost-prince styling, schoolboy proportions, modern idol polish, or shifting apron/shirt construction.

## Whole-Body Anatomical Topology — Absolute

Every visible character must remain one physically coherent human body.

Reject:
- extra/missing arms, hands, legs, feet, ears, fingers, shoulders
- duplicated limbs/body parts
- detached/floating hands/feet/hair masses
- impossible elbow/knee/wrist/ankle/shoulder/hip articulation
- hand or arm emerging from wrong side of torso
- lower body that cannot connect to visible pelvis
- close-up limb incompatible with adjacent body pose

Partial occlusion is allowed only when hidden connections remain physically possible.

## Limb Ownership / Reverse-Shot Identity — Absolute

Continuity-critical close-up limbs belong to the same character and same anatomical side established by adjacent views. Screen-left/right may reverse; anatomical ownership may not.

Glove, sleeve, skin, watch/ring/bracelet, scar, apron, and adjacent body cues remain compatible with ownership.

Production-only identities such as `NOTE HAND`, `TONGS HAND`, `NOTEBOOK HAND`, `FLASHLIGHT HAND`, `TRAY HAND`, `WINDOW HAND`, `PENCIL HAND`, or `TOKEN HAND` are never reader-facing text but may be used internally where needed.

## Wardrobe / Local-Color Continuity — Absolute

Within one continuous scene, clothing construction and signature colors remain the same physical items unless a legal cut or scripted change explicitly establishes another state.

Lighting may change value/saturation naturally, but it may not change identity hue/material.

Examples:
- Daniel's slate-blue jacket must not become black/navy/brown from shot to shot
- Tomas's jade hair cord must remain jade-family, not red/blue/gold
- Tomas's cream shirt and brown apron remain stable within the midnight meeting
- ivory Arturo suit remains an ivory linen suit when later canonically visible

## Speaking / Listening / Text Ownership — Absolute

Dialogue ownership must agree with acting.

Reject:
- listener visibly mouthing another character's line
- two characters visibly speaking one non-overlapping line
- narration/internal thought/station announcement/physical handwriting being acted as human spoken dialogue
- offscreen Lilia suddenly acquiring an on-screen speaking body in Chapter 1 without the strip explicitly establishing her

## Environment Canonical Authority — Absolute

Recurring locations require approved environment canonicals. Exact committed WebP files control geometry and layout.

They control:
- station/cafe footprint
- doors/windows/routes
- counter and service-window relationship
- fixed architectural landmarks
- recurring furniture/fixture placement when canonicalized
- materials/color identity
- physically possible reverse angles and camera axes
- era-specific normal states

A strip may simplify background detail for close-ups but may not invent a different room/station layout.

## Dual-Era Architecture — Absolute

1986 Cafe Siete and 2026 Cafe Siete are the **same underlying physical structure**.

Same for San Aurelio Junction.

Preserve permanent structural relationships across eras. Allowed differences include age, damage, scorch, grime, removed/replaced furniture, redevelopment barriers, operational versus abandoned equipment, lighting, signs, crowds, ordinary historically plausible changes, and deliberately documented later renovation.

Do not mirror or redesign the building simply because one era needs a prettier composition.

## Service Window — Critical Environment Lock

The service window is the most important recurring architectural motif:
- narrow
- between kitchen and counter
- same structural opening in both eras
- suitable for plate/note/hands, not full body
- 1986 intact/polished state
- 2026 ruined/charred-lower-edge state as established
- must preserve hinge/slide/opening mechanism once canonicalized

Hands may approach/cross the threshold only as allowed by current story rules. Do not enlarge the opening to stage romance.

## Core Object Canonical Authority — Absolute

Recurring plot-critical objects use approved canonicals. Preserve proportions, construction, materials, markings, and state vocabulary.

Chapter 1 priority objects include:
- Cafe Siete brass token marked `7`
- brass-rimmed cafe clock
- Daniel's archival notebook/pencil system
- Tomas's brass pastry tongs
- exchanged paper/sleeve material family when exact identity matters

The same physical object remains recognizable through time-aging changes. Do not promote a generated redesign into canon.

## Time-Slip Object Transformation Language

### Forward: 1986 → 2026

Transformation is physical aging of the SAME object:
- paper: yellowing, fading, brittleness, staining
- pastry: drying, shrinking, cracking, dulling
- ceramic: aging/yellowing/crazing where appropriate
- photo: fading/warping
- metal: tarnish/oxidation

Do not dissolve one object and spawn a visually unrelated replacement.

### Backward: 2026 → 1986

Object remains same object, initially unnaturally new for period. Do not magically redesign modern manufacture into an old-fashioned substitute unless script later establishes that behavior.

## Clock / Connection Visual Logic — Absolute

Clock state is story evidence.

For Chapter 1:
- pre-activation 2026: `12:07`
- activation: snap to `12:00`
- progress: 12:01, 12:02, 12:03, 12:04, 12:05, 12:06, 12:07
- post-close station clock remains `12:07`

Do not skip, repeat, reverse, or reset a minute without the locked script.

Connection deterioration is shown by real story states—flicker, instability, disappearing 1986 light, threshold behavior—not generic neon portal effects.

## Physical Text

Story-critical text must remain on its real carrier:
- `TOMAS` scratched into painted 2026 glass
- `FIRE STARTS IN THE SERVICE CORRIDOR.` scratched below it
- Daniel's handwritten identity and warning text on actual paper
- Tomas's `I will bring better bread.` on the same returned paper
- clock numerals/time on the real clock face

Text does not float in the room as UI or hologram.

## Materials — Matte Unless Physical Cause Requires Otherwise

Rain may make exterior surfaces/clothing locally wet, but avoid glamour wetness and mirror streets. Glass may carry restrained rain/sheens without readable reflected duplicate humans. Brass may have simple flat highlights but not mirror chrome.

Steam, flour dust, archive dust, and smoke residue are restrained physical atmosphere, not cinematic volumetric effects.

## Lighting

Lighting serves story-state readability.

2026 abandoned station/cafe:
- cold restrained practical/flashlight/moonlit darkness as physically available
- readable flat shapes
- no unexplained film lighting

1986 active cafe:
- ordinary warm practical cafe bulbs
- flat warm local value shift, not bloom-heavy amber cinema

Connection-open shared threshold:
- each era retains its own plausible lighting while boundary disagreement is readable
- do not flood Daniel with giant orange light or Tomas with blue sci-fi rim light merely to show time difference

12:06 collapse:
- 1986 practical lights may go out sequentially as scripted
- no decorative lightning/portal beams

12:07 closure:
- return to established abandoned 2026 baseline

Light sources must physically exist.

## Composition

Use:
- full-width station/cafe establishing art
- medium conversational compositions
- close reaction shots
- narrow clue/clock/paper inserts
- tall service-window reveals
- borderless environment/threshold art where useful
- meaningful negative space for time pressure or silence

Avoid printed-page grids and repeated equal-size boxes.

A close-up must preserve enough context to prove limb/object ownership when continuity depends on it.

## Reflection / Shadow Ban

Unless specifically scripted, do not invent readable reflected duplicate people, ghost faces, shadow figures, or mystery silhouettes in station glass, pastry case, windows, glossy tile, or metal. Reflections/shadows obey real current light and geometry.

## Final Rejection Gate

Reject a strip if:
- character identity drifts
- anatomy breaks
- landmark/anatomical side changes
- pose/wardrobe/performance resets without cause
- architecture differs between views/eras without canon reason
- service-window geometry changes
- clock time is wrong
- object provenance/aging is wrong
- same-object close-up creates a duplicate
- exact written text is wrong/unreadable/floating
- dialogue/audio ownership is unclear
- physical contact at threshold is impossible
- camera axis/eyeline contradicts geography
- lettering hides continuity proof
- dead space is purposeless
- any production metadata appears in reader art
- output becomes photoreal, 3D, glossy, cinematic, painterly, airbrushed, bloom-heavy, or over-rendered

Style wording alone is never sufficient. Rendered images and final stitched chapter require actual visual inspection.
